# Student Planner UX Research Case Study

An exploratory UX research case study investigating how college students manage academic planning, identify challenges in their existing workflows, and interact with digital planning tools.

## Project Overview

Students often rely on multiple tools and workflows to manage assignments, deadlines, schedules, and academic responsibilities.

This project explored how students currently approach academic planning, where friction occurs in their workflows, and what opportunities exist to improve the planning experience through user-centered design.

The project combines qualitative interviews, survey research, and competitive analysis to identify recurring user needs and translate research findings into design recommendations.

## Research Questions

The research focused on questions including:

- How do students currently organize their academic responsibilities?
- What tools and strategies do they use to manage assignments and deadlines?
- What challenges or frustrations occur within their existing planning workflows?
- What features or improvements could better support students' planning needs?
- Where are there opportunities to reduce friction and improve the overall experience?

## Research Methods

The project used multiple research methods to develop a broader understanding of student planning behavior:

### User Interviews

Conducted interviews with **five students** to explore:

- Current planning habits
- Tools and systems used
- Common frustrations
- Strategies for managing competing responsibilities
- Desired improvements

### Survey Research

A survey was used to gather additional perspectives on:

- Planning behaviors
- Tool usage
- Common challenges
- Preferences and needs

### Competitive Analysis

Existing student planning and productivity tools were reviewed to identify:

- Common features
- Design patterns
- Potential gaps
- Opportunities for differentiation

## Research Process

The research followed a user-centered process:

1. Define the research questions
2. Identify participants and gather user perspectives
3. Conduct interviews and collect survey responses
4. Analyze recurring patterns and pain points
5. Compare findings with existing solutions
6. Identify user needs and opportunities
7. Translate findings into design recommendations

## Key Findings

The research identified several recurring themes related to academic planning, including:

- Difficulty maintaining information across multiple tools
- Challenges prioritizing competing academic responsibilities
- The need for clearer visibility into upcoming deadlines
- Friction associated with keeping planning systems updated
- Opportunities to make planning more personalized and easier to maintain

These findings were used to identify areas where a student planning experience could better support users' existing workflows.

## User Needs

Based on the research, users would benefit from planning tools that:

- Provide a clear overview of upcoming responsibilities
- Reduce the need to maintain information across multiple systems
- Make priorities and deadlines easier to understand
- Support flexible planning rather than requiring a rigid workflow
- Minimize the effort required to keep information current

## Design Recommendations

The research findings informed design recommendations focused on:

- Clear deadline and task visibility
- Prioritization of academic responsibilities
- Simplifying planning workflows
- Reducing unnecessary interaction and maintenance
- Supporting different student planning strategies

The recommendations were developed from the identified user needs rather than assuming that a single planning workflow would work for every student.

## Research-to-Design Connection

A central goal of the project was to connect research findings to design decisions.

Rather than beginning with a predetermined solution, the process used observed user behaviors and reported challenges to identify opportunities for improvement.

This helped establish a workflow of:

**Research → Findings → User Needs → Design Opportunities → Recommendations**

## Limitations

This was an exploratory UX research project with a small participant sample.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as directional rather than representative of the broader college student population.

Future research could expand the participant pool, increase participant diversity, conduct usability testing, and validate the identified needs through behavioral data.
